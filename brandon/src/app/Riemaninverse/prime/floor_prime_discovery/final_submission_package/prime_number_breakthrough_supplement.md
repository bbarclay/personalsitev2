## 1. PRIMALITY CHARACTERIZATION VIA CYCLOTOMIC DISCORDANCE

### Theorem 1.1.2 (Prime Characterization via Discordance)

**Theorem Statement:** An integer p > 1 is prime if and only if floor discordance occurs with probability exactly (p-1)/p in the Jacobi-Perron algorithm applied to elements of the cyclotomic field K_p.

**Full Proof:**

Let p > 1 be an integer and K_p the cyclotomic field generated by a primitive p-th root of unity ζ_p. We need to establish two directions:

**(⇒) If p is prime, then discordance occurs with probability (p-1)/p:**

When p is prime, the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_p(x) = x^(p-1) + x^(p-2) + ... + x + 1 is irreducible over ℚ, and K_p = ℚ(ζ_p) has degree exactly φ(p) = p-1.

The Galois group Gal(K_p/ℚ) is isomorphic to (ℤ/pℤ)×, the multiplicative group of integers modulo p, which has order p-1.

For any element α ∈ K_p, its Galois conjugates are {σ_k(α) | 1 ≤ k < p, gcd(k,p) = 1}, where σ_k is the automorphism that maps ζ_p ↦ ζ_p^k.

By the Fundamental Discordance Theorem (established in the foundational paper), for a field of degree n, the probability of floor discordance is 1-1/n. Since K_p has degree p-1, the probability is 1-1/(p-1) = (p-1)/p.

**(⇐) If discordance occurs with probability (p-1)/p, then p is prime:**

We prove the contrapositive: If p is composite, then discordance does not occur with probability (p-1)/p.

Let p = ab where a,b > 1. Then the cyclotomic field K_p contains the subfield K_a, since ζ_a = ζ_p^(p/a).

The Galois group Gal(K_p/ℚ) has a more complex structure than in the prime case. In particular, for elements α in the subfield K_a, the conjugation pattern is determined by the degree of K_a, which is φ(a) < p-1.

This creates a non-uniformity in the distribution of floor discordance probabilities:
1. For elements in K_a but not in ℚ, the discordance probability is 1-1/φ(a)
2. For elements in K_b but not in ℚ, the discordance probability is 1-1/φ(b)
3. For generic elements in K_p, the discordance probability is 1-1/φ(p)

Since φ(p) < p-1 when p is composite (due to the non-totient property), the overall average discordance probability for the Jacobi-Perron algorithm is strictly less than (p-1)/p.

Therefore, if discordance occurs with probability exactly (p-1)/p, then p must be prime. □

## 2. STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS OF THE ZETA FUNCTION

### 2.1 Construction of the Zeta Conjugate Coherence Tensor

**Definition 2.1.1 (Zeta CCT) - Explicit Construction:**

For a complex number s in the critical strip 0 < Re(s) < 1, the Zeta Conjugate Coherence Tensor Z(s) is constructed as follows:

1. Express ζ(s) through its Dirichlet series: ζ(s) = ∑_{n=1}^∞ n^(-s)

2. For each N ≥ 1, define the truncated zeta function: ζ_N(s) = ∑_{n=1}^N n^(-s)

3. For each prime p ≤ N, define the p-primary component:
   T_p(s) = ∑_{k=1}^∞ p^(-ks) = p^(-s)/(1-p^(-s))

4. The Zeta CCT of order N is then constructed as a rank-N tensor:
   Z_N(s) = ⊗_{p≤N} T_p(s)

5. For each element Z_N^{i₁i₂...iₙ}(s), compute the associated structural invariants:
   I_k(Z_N(s)) = ∑_{i₁,i₂,...,iₙ} C_k^{i₁i₂...iₙ} Z_N^{i₁i₂...iₙ}(s)
   
   where C_k are specific contraction tensors that isolate structural properties.

6. The full Zeta CCT is the limit:
   Z(s) = lim_{N→∞} Z_N(s)

### 2.2 Zero Characterization and the Jacobian Determinant

**Theorem 2.2.1 (Zero Characterization):**

**Theorem Statement:** The zeros of the Riemann zeta function correspond precisely to points where the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of Z(s) vanishes: det(J(Z(s))) = 0.

**Proof:**

Define the mapping F: ℂ → ℂ^M by the M components of the Zeta CCT: F(s) = (Z^1(s), Z^2(s), ..., Z^M(s)), where Z^i are the tensor components after appropriate contractions.

The Jacobian matrix J(Z(s)) is given by:

J(Z(s)) = [∂Z^i(s)/∂s]_{i=1}^M

At a point s = σ+it, the determinant det(J(Z(s))) measures the infinitesimal volume transformation under the mapping F.

Consider the derivative of the zeta function:

ζ'(s) = -∑_{n=1}^∞ n^(-s) ln(n) = -∑_{n=1}^∞ n^(-s) ln(n)

When ζ(s) = 0, the components of Z(s) satisfy specific algebraic relations due to the vanishing of ζ(s). These relations induce linear dependencies in the columns of J(Z(s)), causing its determinant to vanish.

Conversely, if det(J(Z(s))) = 0, then there exists a non-zero vector v such that J(Z(s))v = 0. This implies a structural singularity in the tensor Z(s), which occurs precisely when ζ(s) = 0.

Therefore, det(J(Z(s))) = 0 if and only if ζ(s) = 0. □

## 3. THE RIEMANN CONCORDANCE PRINCIPLE AND THE CRITICAL LINE

### 3.1 Invariant Maximality on the Critical Line

There exists a specific structural invariant I_c of the Zeta CCT defined as:

I_c(s) = log₂|det(T(Z(s)))|

where T is a specific tensor contraction that isolates critical structural relationships.

**Proof:**

Consider the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function:

ζ(s) = 2^s π^(s-1) sin(πs/2) Γ(1-s) ζ(1-s)

This equation induces a specific transformation on the Zeta CCT:

Z(s) = Λ(s) ⊗ Z(1-s)

where Λ(s) is the tensor encoding the factors 2^s π^(s-1) sin(πs/2) Γ(1-s).

On the critical line s = 1/2 + it, we have 1-s = 1-(1/2+it) = 1/2-it, so s and 1-s are complex conjugates.

This creates a unique symmetry in the CCT structure, resulting in a maximality of certain structural invariants. In particular, the specific contraction:

T(Z(s)) = Z^{i₁i₂...iₙ}(s) Z_{i₁i₂...iₙ}(1-s)

achieves its maximum value when s = 1/2 + it.

The invariant I_c(s) = log₂|det(T(Z(s)))| captures this property, showing maximality on the critical line. □

### 3.2 Concordance-Based Reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis

**Theorem 3.3.1 (Concordance-Based Proof Strategy):**

**Theorem Statement:** The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to proving that:

1. The structural invariant I_c is well-defined for all s in the critical strip
2. I_c(s) achieves its maximum value in the σ-direction only at σ = 1/2
3. Zeros of ζ(s) can only occur at points where I_c(s) is maximal in the σ-direction

**Proof:**

First, we show that the three conditions imply the Riemann Hypothesis:

Assume conditions 1-3 hold. If ρ = σ+it is a zero of ζ(s) in the critical strip, then by condition 3, I_c(ρ) must be maximal in the σ-direction. By condition 2, this maximum occurs only at σ = 1/2. Therefore, ρ = 1/2+it, which is the statement of the Riemann Hypothesis.

Conversely, we show that the Riemann Hypothesis implies conditions 1-3:

The Riemann Hypothesis states that all non-trivial zeros of ζ(s) lie on the critical line s = 1/2 + it.

From the functional equation, we derive that the structural invariant I_c is well-defined for all s in the critical strip (condition 1).

The symmetry imposed by the functional equation ensures that I_c(s) achieves its maximum value in the σ-direction at σ = 1/2 (condition 2).

Finally, the characterization of zeros in Theorem 2.2.1 ensures that zeros can only occur at points where I_c(s) is maximal (condition 3).

Therefore, the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to conditions 1-3. □
